📈 1. Markets & Finance

PPI undershoots but the Fed is still on a hiking path; 10Y holds above 5%

🟢 What happened: October PPI rose 0.3% m/m against a 0.4% consensus, and 6.5% y/y, the highest since Nov 2022. CNBC has core at +0.5%. Earlier in the week October CPI printed +0.4% headline and +0.3% core. The 10Y is ~5.15%, the 2Y ~4.87% and the 30Y ~5.48% (+5bp). Futures put ~86–91% odds on a 25bp December hike, with the rest on 50bp.

🟠 Why it matters: The headline miss is not a pivot signal. A hot core reading in the pipeline keeps the December hike live, and the curve is bear-steepening: the long end is moving more than the front end. Long rates above 5% raise the hurdle for equity multiples, LBO financing and CRE refinancing all at once.

🟣 Context & connections: The Fed already hiked in September to 3.75–4.00% and held on Oct 28 with two dissents in favor of hiking. Gov. Waller has floated 50bp if core stays sticky. This is the first hiking cycle since 2022–23, now with oil in the mid-$90s from the Iran war, so the setup looks more like a 1970s-style supply shock than a demand-led overheating. The 30Y near 5.5% is close to 2007 levels.

📘 Key terms: PPI: Producer Price Index, wholesale/input prices, a leading indicator for CPI. Core: excludes volatile food and energy (PPI core also strips trade services). Bear steepening: long yields rise faster than short yields. bp: basis point, 0.01%.

Equities chop: Dow up, Nasdaq soft after Thursday's broad 0.4% decline

🟢 What happened: Thursday closed with the Dow at 52,297 (-0.4%), the S&P 500 at 7,592 (-0.4%) and the Nasdaq at 25,366 (-0.4%). Friday's open was mixed: the Dow was ~+0.3% while the S&P and Nasdaq were flat to lower, as Nvidia slipped again.

🟠 Why it matters: Leadership is rotating away from mega-cap semis. The AI complex has been under pressure since the post-midterm chip rout (AMAT fell 13.6% and the Nasdaq 5.5% on Nov 5, per Trefis), and the Nvidia leadership shock adds a key-person risk discount to the index's largest weight.

🟣 Context & connections: Nvidia's concentration in the S&P makes its governance problem an index-level problem. Next week has Anthropic's IPO, which will absorb roughly $64B of liquidity, plus Nvidia earnings. Together they will test whether the AI trade can hold up with 5% long rates.

📘 Key terms: Key-person risk: valuation exposure to a single executive. Rotation: capital moving between sectors or styles.

🤝 2. Deals & Banking

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO

🟢 What happened: Per Nvidia's 8-K, Huang resigned effective immediately as President, CEO and director. Kress will remain CFO while serving as interim CEO, and Harvey Jones becomes the board's first independent chair. ABC and Al Jazeera tie the exit to reported personal-conduct issues, including an alleged relationship with a person US officials suspect is a Chinese intelligence asset. These are media reports, not company statements. The stock fell more than 13% intraday Thursday before paring losses.

🟠 Why it matters: Huang was a founder and CEO for 33 years. This is the largest unplanned CEO transition in market-cap terms on record. The China angle raises national-security and export-control scrutiny for the world's most strategically important chip company, and it gives the board a governance overhaul to run alongside a CEO search, both internal and external (Ian Buck is a prediction-market favorite).

🟣 Context & connections: Comparable founder exits include Jobs in 2011 (planned, and AAPL was fine) and Kalanick at Uber in 2017 (scandal-driven, with a multi-year governance reset). For deal desks, expect activist and governance-advisory mandates, and possible delays to Nvidia's strategic investment program (it was just in the Agile Robots round). Also expect questions over Nvidia's role in the Anthropic and OpenAI capital stacks.

📘 Key terms: 8-K: SEC filing for material events. Interim CEO: placeholder leader during a search. Independent chair: board chair separate from management, a governance best practice.

Anthropic sets IPO range at $70–78 on 825M shares; pricing expected Nov 18

🟢 What happened: An amended S-1 filed Nov 10 sets a $70–78 range for 825M Class A shares, up to ~$64.4B in proceeds, according to Invezz. Reports point to Nasdaq trading from Nov 19, but the filing does not confirm the date. Separately, OpenAI reportedly raised $15B at a $1.25T valuation, about half of its $30B target (single-source report).

🟠 Why it matters: This would be the largest IPO ever by proceeds, well above Saudi Aramco's ~$29B in 2019. It is a defining league-table event for the underwriting syndicate, and it serves as a live price check on private AI marks across VC and crossover portfolios.

🟣 Context & connections: The deal is launching into 5%+ long yields, a hawkish Fed and a damaged AI-semis tape after the Nvidia news. OpenAI's under-filled private round is a demand data point worth watching. A strong book would reopen the mega-cap tech IPO window for 2027. A broken deal would reprice late-stage private AI.

📘 Key terms: S-1: IPO registration statement. Range: indicative price band set before bookbuilding. Crossover investors: funds that hold both private and public stakes.

Marvell–Lumentum ($115B EV, all-stock) still the year's reference tech deal

🟢 What happened: The deal was announced Nov 2: Lumentum holders receive 4.5 MRVL shares per share. Closing is expected in 1H28, subject to shareholder and antitrust approvals. Per the 8-K, Qatalyst is lead advisor to Marvell (with JPM and GS), and Citi and Evercore advise Lumentum.

🟠 Why it matters: Because it is all-stock, Lumentum holders' value moves with MRVL, which has fallen in the semis drawdown. That makes deal-spread risk a live question. The long closing timeline points to heavy antitrust and CFIUS/SAMR review.

🟣 Context & connections: The deal is a bet on co-packaged optics for AI data centers. On the boutique side, it is a marquee Evercore credit and a Qatalyst win in Centerview's core tech turf.

📘 Key terms: Exchange ratio: fixed shares per target share. EV: enterprise value (equity + net debt). SAMR: China's antitrust regulator.

🏦 3. Private Equity

Shield buys Echodyne for $5.3B with Blackstone financing; Permira takes Flint Group

🟢 What happened: AltAssets reports (Nov 10) that Shield is acquiring radar maker Echodyne for $5.3B, financed by Blackstone. In the same roundup, Permira agreed to buy Flint Group. Details are limited to trade-press headlines.

🟠 Why it matters: Defense tech is now a scaled PE and private-credit asset class. Blackstone providing the financing shows private credit replacing syndicated loans, which matters more with base rates rising.

🟣 Context & connections: It is consistent with this week's defense megarounds (UFORCE, $700M Series A at $6B) and with sovereign rearmament budgets in Europe and the US.

📘 Key terms: Private credit: non-bank direct lending. Sponsor: the PE owner.

Cinven agrees €2.6B Theramex buyout from Carlyle/PAI; PAI buys Elemis from L'Occitane

🟢 What happened: Cinven is acquiring women's-health specialty pharma company Theramex from Carlyle and PAI for about €2.6B. PAI is also buying skincare brand Elemis from L'Occitane. Separately, Mason Wells is running a sale of KJ Holding (Calypso lemonade).

🟠 Why it matters: These are sponsor-to-sponsor exits. Theramex gives Carlyle and PAI distributions (DPI) at a time when LPs are pressing for cash back.

🟣 Context & connections: European mid-to-large-cap is where exits are clearing, while US LBO math is strained by 5%+ rates. Also on the fundraising side: Norwest closed $4B, Ardian raised €1B for semiconductors, and VRS plans more than $750M a year in co-investments.

📘 Key terms: Secondary buyout: PE-to-PE sale. DPI: distributions to paid-in capital, actual cash returned to LPs. Co-invest: LPs investing directly alongside a fund.

🌍 4. Macro & Geopolitics

Oil whipsaws on Iran/Ukraine peace hopes, then on Saudi and Hormuz attacks

🟢 What happened: Brent dropped more than $1 on hopes for ceasefires in both Ukraine and the Iran war. It then gained more than $2 after attacks on Saudi energy sites and explosions on four tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Ukrainian drones also hit Russia's Ust-Luga Baltic port. Brent January is ~$96.50, though it is still headed for a second weekly loss.

🟠 Why it matters: Energy is the main input behind 6.5% PPI. Every leg higher in crude raises the probability of a 50bp Fed move. Meanwhile, Hormuz risk keeps a geopolitical premium in the price that a ceasefire headline can quickly erase.

🟣 Context & connections: China's October crude imports hit their highest level since March but are still down 13% y/y, as Beijing draws on stockpiles. Its demand discipline has been capping prices. The US–China Busan tariff truce was extended to Jan 10, 2027, and Trump and Xi are expected to meet at APEC in Shenzhen (date unconfirmed), then at the G20 in Miami in December.

📘 Key terms: Brent/WTI: global and US crude benchmarks. Risk premium: price above fundamentals for supply-disruption risk. Strait of Hormuz: chokepoint for ~20% of global oil flows.

🏛️ 5. US National Politics

White House moves to block incoming House Democratic investigations

🟢 What happened: The White House counsel warned that aggressive oversight "may cross the line into blatant criminality," per NBC. The counsel's office has added 15 lawyers. Incoming Oversight chair Robert Garcia cited Congress's constitutional investigative authority.

🟠 Why it matters: After the midterms, Democrats control the House, which brings subpoena fights and divided-government gridlock. For markets, the near-term risk is the government funding bill now at the top of Congress's agenda: a shutdown fight would delay economic data releases just before the Fed decides.

🟣 Context & connections: Trump named Stephen Miller chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles, which signals a more confrontational posture. House Republicans narrowly defeated a Massie resolution to impeach Defense Sec. Hegseth, and Jim Jordan becomes House minority leader. The pattern resembles 2019 (Mueller and Ukraine subpoena fights), but with tariff authority already curtailed by the Supreme Court.

📘 Key terms: Oversight: congressional investigative power. CR: continuing resolution, stopgap funding at prior-year levels.

🗽 6. New York Politics

Post-election, Hochul's budget office rejects DSA tax-hike push; pied-à-terre tax goes to Mamdani

🟢 What happened: After Hochul's re-election, budget director Blake Washington rejected calls from Mamdani's DSA allies for tax increases, citing an ~$8B revenue surplus (NY Post via Finwire). Hochul has given the city authority to enact a pied-à-terre tax on luxury second homes. Mamdani says he will keep pressing Albany for income-tax authority on high earners.

🟠 Why it matters: This is the clearest signal on NYC's business and tax climate going into the 2027 session. State income-tax hikes on high earners, the main concern for finance professionals and firm location decisions, look off the table for now. The pied-à-terre tax would hit the Manhattan luxury condo market directly.

🟣 Context & connections: Mamdani's universal childcare agenda depends on Albany funding, and Hochul holds the purse. Their alliance through the campaign (Siena found his endorsement a net positive, 45–30) now meets real budget tradeoffs. The pied-à-terre tax has been proposed and dropped since 2014 (it was killed in 2019 in favor of the mansion tax).

📘 Key terms: Pied-à-terre tax: annual levy on high-value non-primary residences. DSA: Democratic Socialists of America.
